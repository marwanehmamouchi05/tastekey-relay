# Judge walkthrough

Open [Personal](https://tastekey.52.47.205.18.sslip.io/personal/discover) and [Studio](https://tastekey.52.47.205.18.sslip.io/studio/onboarding) in your own browser. Each Studio visitor gets an empty private workspace. No shared judge account is needed.

## Personal: taste with control

1. Search for Interstellar and explicitly select the intended Qloo result. Add a design taste such as luxury architecture. Search selections carry actual cultural entity IDs; free-text tastes remain useful locally but do not impersonate resolved entities.
2. Answer an adaptive follow-up. Inspect Taste DNA and the accessible list. Unanswered hypotheses remain unverified. Reject an unwanted interpretation.
3. Set a practical hotel limit independently of design taste. To exercise live AI, enter “I love luxury architecture. My hotel budget is $60 a night. Keep my movie tastes.” and explicitly choose Interpret with language model. Inspect the proposed changes, then apply them. Only the correction text is submitted for this step.
4. In Relay, grant selected fields to entertainment, shopping or travel. Inspect the disclosure preview, real Qloo results and expiry. Revoke permission and confirm that results disappear.
5. Travel destinations are inspiration. If you have a genuinely checked accommodation quote, enter its public source, checked time, stay date and USD nightly price. Filtering respects destination, freshness and declared budget. Do not invent a quote to represent actual availability. Shopping's sample catalog is illustrative.
6. In Proof, export or delete your profile. Common-pool observations are exploratory self-reports, not a controlled study.

## Studio: evidence to an experiment

1. Create an explicitly synthetic cinema brief in Casablanca, with a 500 MAD pilot budget and a one-evening capacity limit. Review Interstellar from cultural search before saving.
2. Run Cultural Analysis. Optional AI advisor selection explicitly sends the brief and cultural evidence to Bedrock. Inspect entity IDs, location signal, investigation trace, limits and the four grounded proposals. Allocations are planning bounds, not supplier quotes or financial forecasts.
3. Revise, accept or reject a proposal. Create an action plan from an accepted proposal, then edit its owner, date, objective and dependencies.
4. In Scenarios, compare Rabat and 250 MAD. The city triggers a new location signal and the budget recalculates allocations. Both input snapshots remain attached to their analyses.
5. Outcomes are owner-reported. Enter a result only if it happened; label any test entry synthetic. Later investigations use these reports and rejections under explicit planning rules.
6. Human evaluation stores anchored ratings and rationale against an exact analysis ID. An alias does not establish independent participation or blinding.
7. On the dashboard, generate and download a private recovery code. Restoring it in another browser signs out older sessions. Save exports separately; the code does not sync Personal's local graph.

## Optional consented audience connection

Create an invitation in Studio's audience view. Open its link in Personal Proof, preview the named business, choose broad categories and explicitly consent. The request contains the invitation, selected category names and consent only. It excludes graph nodes, entity IDs, budget and contact information. Withdraw from the participant view. Reports stay suppressed below ten active browser-session contributions; small groups and complements are also suppressed. Do not create fake participants to imply real demand. Automated tests exercise synthetic thresholds; no real customer dataset is claimed.
